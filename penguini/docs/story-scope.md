# PENGUINI — Full Story Scope

The complete narrative for Cold City. This is the source for the `scenes.json`
dialogue file as each chapter gets built. Nothing here is written into the game
until its chapter comes up in the build order at the bottom.

---

## Logline

A broke penguin rapper, kicked out of his crew and dumped by the only person who
believed in him, claws his way up Cold City's criminal ladder to get rich — and
discovers the bears at the top aren't selling drugs, they're selling his
neighborhood.

## Theme

There are two ways to make it out of Cold City: build something, or take
something. Penguini spends the whole game convinced they're the same thing. They
aren't, and the ending is determined by which one he was actually doing.

## Tone rules

1. **The world is ridiculous. The stakes are played straight.** Bears in
   tracksuits, seal cops eating on cruiser hoods, a rap battle in a fish market.
   Nobody in a story scene ever winks.
2. **No villain thinks they're a villain.** Every bear has a reason and most of
   them are good ones.
3. **Money is never the reward.** Every time Penguini gets paid, something else
   gets a little worse. The player should start to feel this around chapter 8
   without ever being told.

---

## The city

Cold City is built on an ice shelf, four districts stacked from the water up.

- **Igloo Row (Block 9)** — where Penguini lives. Low domed housing, meltwater in
  the streets, family-run everything. Being demolished, block by block,
  throughout the game.
- **The Boardwalk** — fish markets, arcades, the Krill King, cheap studios.
  Kodi's territory. Loud, young, violent.
- **The Docks** — smuggling, cargo, the harbor rigs. Bera's. The oldest money in
  the city.
- **The Meridian** — glass towers, forty floors of bear penthouses looking down
  at everything. Bruin above, Whitlock's offices at the top.

The camera should be able to see the Meridian towers from every single street in
Igloo Row. That's not a level design note, it's the thesis.

## Cast

### Penguini

A genuinely talented rapper who has never once finished anything. Charming,
quick, allergic to the small unglamorous step. His flaw isn't greed — it's that
he'd rather be the guy in the story than the guy doing the work. Everything that
happens to him follows from that.

### Cindy Marlow

One floor up on Block 9. Pharmacy counter by day, marine systems certification at
night. The only character who ever listened to his tape all the way through. Her
full arc is in the separate Cindy document — seven scenes, and she never comes
back.

### Tuck

The one Frostbite Boy who walks out with him. Loyal, funny, not smart, absolutely
fearless in the exact situations where fear would be useful. He's the comic
relief for the first ten chapters, and then in chapter 12 he gets badly hurt on a
job Penguini talked him into, and he is never funny again. That's the moment the
game stops being a fantasy.

### DJ Fathom

Old walrus who runs a basement studio under the Boardwalk. Was somebody thirty
years ago. Never lectures Penguini, just keeps the booth unlocked and the light
on. Represents the slow road. Every chapter he asks the same question — *"You
gonna finish it this time?"* — and the player's answer is tracked.

### Detective Bask

Cold City Marine Patrol. The seal who ends up on Penguini's case. Not corrupt,
not stupid, not a joke. She's right about him the entire game and he keeps
proving her right. Their scenes should feel like two people who'd have liked each
other in a different life.

### The Bears

**Bruin** — Meridian Tower. Old money, corporate, three layers of legitimate
business between him and anything illegal. Polite to the point of menace. Never
raises his voice. Wants to be seen as a businessman, and the fact that he isn't
one is the only thing that can hurt him.

**Kodi** — the Boardwalk. Young, loud, reckless, closest to the street and the
most dangerous to be near. He's the one who gives Penguini his first real shot,
and he's the one who sets him up in chapter 13. Wants Bruin's chair and doesn't
have the patience to earn it.

**Mama Bera** — the Docks. Matriarch, four generations of smuggling. Treats
everyone like family, including people she's about to have killed. The only bear
who's ever kind to Penguini, which makes her the most frightening one in the
game. Wants the family business to survive her.

**Whitlock** — the top floor. A bear who went completely legitimate: developer,
city councilman, foundation on his name. He has never broken a law. He is
demolishing Igloo Row to build luxury towers and he's doing it entirely through
zoning permits. **He's the real antagonist, and the player shouldn't realize it
until act three.** Wants the city itself.

## Act One — Rock Bottom (Chapters 1–5)

**Ch 1 — Cold Open.** Behind the Krill King. Slick kicks him out of the Frostbite
Boys. *(This is Phase 1 of the build.)*

**Ch 2 — Nowhere Else.** 1:40am at Cindy's door. He arrives expecting rescue and
gets the truth instead. She ends it.

**Ch 3 — Seventeen Seconds.** The low point. The map greys out. Two locations,
one action, then Sleep. Voicemail. This runs three in-game days and should be the
shortest and heaviest chapter in the game.

**Ch 4 — Rent Is Due Friday.** He gets up. Three leads on the back of a takeout
menu: sell tapes on the Boardwalk, run a package for Kodi, boost a snowmobile off
Pier 3. Player picks one and the other two stay available. First Cash, first
Heat, first Cred.

**Ch 5 — The Basement.** Meets Fathom. Meets Tuck outside the Krill King, also
freshly out. The three threads of the game are now open: the studio, the crew,
the bears.

## Act Two — The Climb (Chapters 6–14)

**Ch 6 — Errands.** Small jobs for Kodi. Fun, low stakes, good money.
Deliberately the most enjoyable stretch of the game.

**Ch 7 — The Verse.** Fathom gets him ten minutes on a local station. Player
chooses: use the slot, or take a job that conflicts with it. First real fork
between the two roads.

**Ch 8 — The Rig Job.** First proper heist — cargo off the harbor rigs. Big
payday. Also the first time Detective Bask says his name out loud.

**Ch 9 — The Envelope.** He goes to Cindy with four thousand dollars. She takes
the eight hundred and forty she's owed and pushes the rest back. *"I'm not taking
bear money, Ini."* The hinge of the entire game.

**Ch 10 — Upstairs.** Bruin summons him to the Meridian. Cold, courteous, forty
degrees colder than the street. Penguini has been noticed, which is exactly what
he wanted and the worst thing that's happened to him.

**Ch 11 — Family Dinner.** Mama Bera at the Docks. She feeds him. She asks about
his mother. She offers him work that he cannot refuse without insulting her, and
the player will feel the trap close.

**Ch 12 — Deep Water.** The job Penguini talks Tuck into. It goes wrong. Tuck
lives and doesn't recover. No music over this one.

**Ch 13 — The Setup.** Kodi burns him to the Seals to clear his own name. Chase
sequence, maximum Heat, Bask nearly has him. Penguini survives on his own for the
first time in the game.

**Ch 14 — What He's Owed.** Payback on Kodi — and the player chooses how far.
Kill him, ruin him, or hand him to Bask. This choice locks large parts of act
three.

## Act Three — The Tower (Chapters 15–20)

**Ch 15 — Notice to Vacate.** Block 9 gets demolition notices. Cindy's building.
Fathom's studio. The Krill King. Signed by a foundation nobody's heard of.

**Ch 16 — Whitlock.** Penguini meets him at a ribbon-cutting. He's charming,
generous, funds a youth music program, and he has never broken a single law. He
offers Penguini a job doing outreach — being the face of the thing that's killing
his neighborhood.

**Ch 17 — The Only Way Up.** Bruin explains that Whitlock is untouchable through
crime, because crime is beneath him. The only thing that can take him down is
paperwork — and the paperwork is in the Meridian.

**Ch 18 — Glacier Ave, 6am.** Quiet chapter. He sees Cindy across four lanes
coming off a shift, work jacket, her name stitched on it. Approach or don't.

**Ch 19 — The Last Session.** Fathom asks the question one final time: *"You
gonna finish it this time?"* Whether the player has a finished track by now
determines which ending is available.

**Ch 20 — The Meridian Job.** The final heist. Not money — files. The zoning
fraud that stops the demolition. Penguini can walk out with the evidence, or with
the vault, and he cannot carry both.

## Endings

Determined by two hidden values: **Built** (finished tracks, Fathom sessions,
Tuck cared for, Cindy's flag) versus **Took** (bear jobs, Heat, the Kodi choice).

**The Artist** — *Built high.* He takes the files, not the money. Block 9 stands.
He's broke. The track drops and it's real and it's small and it's his. Cindy
comes to him in the last scene — not to get back together, but because he's the
only person who can help her brother, and she hates that that's true, and she
asks anyway. He's finally the penguin she spent two years believing in. Credits
roll without giving him the reward, because being that person is the reward.

**The Kingpin** — *Took high.* He takes the vault. He gets the chair at the
Meridian, the tracksuit, the view. Block 9 comes down and he watches it from
forty floors up. Rooftop party, and Cindy is across the room working it. She
looks at him for two seconds and leaves early. No music cue. No achievement. He
has everything he said he wanted in chapter 1.

**The Ghost** — *Heat maxed.* Bask gets him in the last chapter. The interrogation
room is the last playable scene, and she isn't triumphant — she's tired, and a
little sad, and she tells him he was never going to be the exception. Eight
years. Fathom writes. Cindy doesn't.

**The Long Way** — *hidden, requires Built high AND every Fathom session
completed AND the mid-game choice to skip the chapter-7 job.* Penguini never does
the Meridian job at all. He's still broke, still on Block 9, and the last shot is
him walking into the booth. Hardest to get, shortest ending, the one people will
make videos about.

## Mapping to the build

Don't write all of this into `scenes.json` at once. The order that keeps the
project alive:

1. **Chapter 1** — the vertical slice. One block, one scene.
2. **Chapters 2–4** — proves the dialogue system, the stat system, and the
   low-point sequence all work.
3. **Chapter 9** — build the Envelope scene early even though it lands mid-game.
   If that scene works, the game works.
4. Everything else in order.

Each chapter is roughly one scene file plus one mission. Twenty chapters is a real
game and a multi-year project. Five chapters is a demo people can play and share,
and that's the thing worth aiming at this year.
